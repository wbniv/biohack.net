# Korea and Viet Nam border-entry requirements

Verified against official sources on **7 August 2026** for the planned October–November 2026 journey. This snapshot assumes travel on a **United States passport** for tourism. Recheck if the passport, dates, purpose, or ports change.

## South Korea

- A U.S. passport holder may enter visa-free for tourism or business for up to 90 days.
- The passport must be valid at the time of entry; the current U.S. government country page does not specify an additional remaining-validity period.
- U.S. citizens are temporarily exempt from K-ETA through **31 December 2026**, so the planned October 2026 arrival falls within the exemption. A voluntary K-ETA is unnecessary for this trip.
- A traveller without a valid K-ETA should submit Korea's **free e-Arrival Card within three days before arrival**. Prepare the passport, email address, arrival flight/date, and Korean stay address. Save the submission number and an offline copy.
- Carry the Seoul accommodation details and onward Seoul/Incheon–Da Nang booking. These are sensible supporting evidence even though the cited pages do not impose a universal printed-proof rule.
- Use only `e-arrivalcard.go.kr`; the Korean government warns about imitation sites.

## Viet Nam

- Apply while outside Viet Nam through the Ministry of Public Security's official portal, `evisa.gov.vn`.
- An e-visa can be issued for no more than **90 days**, as single or multiple entry. The current plan has one Viet Nam entry and no departure during the Thai-visa wait, so a **US$25 single-entry e-visa** is sufficient. Multiple entry costs US$50. Fees are non-refundable if refused.
- Use **Da Nang International Airport**, which appears on the official list of permitted e-visa entry/exit checkpoints. Settle the intended exit airport before applying and make the application, approval, and actual itinerary agree.
- Enter the passport identity, requested validity dates, intended length of stay, first Viet Nam address and current ward, purpose, and entry/exit gates exactly. Cross-check the issued document against the passport and tickets before travel.
- Keep at least six months of passport validity on entry. Official Vietnamese pages are not perfectly consistent about where this is stated, so the plan deliberately uses the conservative six-month threshold rather than risking airline or border refusal.
- Print the approved e-visa and keep offline copies. Complete the official pre-arrival form recommended by the immigration portal before travel.
- The e-visa permits travel to the checkpoint; final admission remains an immigration decision.

## Recheck triggers

Recheck the official sources immediately before each application and again 72 hours before travel, especially if:

- arrival moves beyond 31 December 2026;
- the passport or nationality changes;
- the trip becomes work, study, or another non-tourist purpose;
- the Viet Nam entry or exit gate changes;
- the Viet Nam stay may exceed the approved e-visa dates.
